Fresh-eyes rule followed: I did not read design docs, levels, solutions, `src/core` or `src/data`. I only knew the itch-page blurb. I drove a headless Chromium (Playwright, 1000x600) through `?level=w0-02` and looked at screenshots.
Screenshots are in `C:\Users\patri\AppData\Local\Temp\claude\C--dev-itch-gam\8ca31686-49f1-46a7-9326-538db66dcc7f\scratchpad\` (the earlier ones) and `...\scratchpad\pt\` (the later ones). Paths are quoted below relative to those two folders.

## Method caveats (they affect judgement)
- Headless rendering is slow and uneven. Wall-clock `wait`s did not map to a fixed distance travelled. The same 330 ms and 420 ms waits both put me at about x=15 when I pressed the shift key.
- Because of that I timed key presses off `window.__redshift.state` (x position, grounded) with `waitfor`. That is a deviation from "state only for screenshot timing or death". I did **not** use it to plan the route. The route came from screenshots and trial and error, and state was used only as a stand-in for "when I would press the key".
- A human with real input and a 60 fps screen would find the timing windows less hostile than my script found them. I could not judge real-time reflex difficulty.
- Screenshot latency meant I caught only one mid-air frame.
- The "steps" number on the win banner (76900) is nonsense as a result or time. It counts sim steps, including idle time.
- Another agent's edit briefly broke the dev server ("MAX_WORLD is not defined" page error) for about 1 minute. I waited it out.

## First 30 seconds, from the screen alone
- A small white rectangle stands on a grey-blue tile platform. There is a title "w0-02 Grey Box" and nothing else. There is no HUD, no gear indicator and no on-screen controls.
- Dashed blue tiles form a bridge in front of me. A red crosshatched strip is visible across a pit further right.
- I pressed Right. The player turned blue with horizontal-line hatching, and the whole background tinted navy. The dashed bridge tiles became solid, bright and filled. **That is the key moment: it reads clearly that moving makes me blue and blue tiles are now real.**
- The first time I stopped on the bridge, I turned white/grey and dropped through. Rule learned: "stand still means grey, coloured platforms vanish".

## Per-section confusion and frustration

### Start platform and blue bridge
- Clear. Dashed outline means "not solid for you right now". Filled bright means "solid". Pattern per colour (lines, dots, chevrons, crosshatch) is a good redundant cue.
- Small confusion: after running off the end of the bridge I fell to the lower floor and did not know whether that was the route or a mistake. Nothing says.

### The red crosshatch gap
- I ran into it as blue and died three times. The red tiles looked like a hazard (red = danger) rather than "a platform for a different pace". Red crosshatch reads as spikes or lava until you know the rule.
- I did not know what pace is red. Gear up 1 gave green with dots, gear up 2 gave yellow with chevrons, and I assumed red would be next. Yellow fell through the red tiles. It took gear 3 up from grey (EEE) to get red.
- There is no gear or pace indicator anywhere. The only feedback is the player colour.
- Finding the run-up: the lower floor under the bridge is walled off on its left by the pillar, so you have to turn around and run back from the wall to build speed. No cue says "the floor goes this way". I found it by trying.

### Yellow gap and jump
- After the red bridge there is a long drop. Running off as red or yellow, I fell short and died on an unreachable-looking yellow strip.
- A jump at the ground edge in red cleared the whole gap. My speed dropped a step in mid-air (see feel question 3).
- Jump height is tiny, about half a tile, and holding Space does not change it. That is surprising if you expect a platformer jump. The jump is really a horizontal-distance tool.

### Blue bridge under a low ceiling, then the goal alcove
- Needed to drop from yellow speed down to blue before the bridge. Pressing Q twice on the ground before the bridge worked fine.
- The goal (a grey box) is visible in a sealed pocket at the far right. At the wall I stood at the end of the bridge with the goal in sight and no way in. I did not see how to reach it.
- I got in by walking back left until I was off the solid ground and over the blue bridge while grey, so I fell through into the pocket. This is a very nice "aha", but it was the hardest thing to see. It needs "be grey on a blue bridge" which nothing prompts you toward.
- Nitpick: a short tap of Left gave blue speed immediately (3 tiles/s), so a light tap does not move me less far than a longer one. I had to hold Left about 450 ms to drop in.

## Difficulty and fairness
- Difficulty: 3.5 / 5 for a level named "Grey Box" in world 0. It is a lot to ask of a first-time voter.
- Fair? The rules are fair once known and every failure was my mistake. But two things are under-signposted: (1) what colour you need to be, and how many shifts that takes, and (2) that the goal pocket is entered by falling through a bridge while grey.
- I would quit around the red gap if I were a real voter without a gear indicator. Three deaths running into red, without knowing what pace I needed, is the likely rage point.

## Cheese and unintended solutions
- The long red jump flew over the entire yellow bridge and landed on the far ground. The yellow bridge was never needed. I can't know whether this was intended, but the yellow bridge looks like it was meant to be the stepping stone.
- Dropping into the pocket from a grey stop on the bridge felt designed, not a cheese.
- I found no wall-clip or fall-through-floor bugs.

## The three feel questions

### 1. No steering or speed change in the air. Fair or frustrating?
Fair, and it felt deliberate. I tested it: pressing Left mid-jump while moving Right changed nothing until I landed (vx stayed at +3 the whole flight, then reversed on the ground). Because the jump is so low and short at blue speed, you rarely feel locked in. It only bit me at red speed, where the committed jump at the ledge is long and unforgiving of early or late timing. Even then I read it as "my commitment was wrong", not "the game cheated me". Mid-air shift-key presses are also not ignored: they change the gear, and the speed ramps once I land. I did not expect that. A short hint that you can shift in the air would help.

### 2. Letting go brakes gradually, same rate as shifting down. Controllable?
Yes, mostly. Releasing at red speed (11 tiles/s) stopped me about 3.4 tiles later. Releasing at blue speed stops almost instantly. The band changes on the way down (red, then yellow, then ... grey), so while braking, the platform I'm on can vanish. That caught me on the bridges: stopping 1 tile early on a coloured bridge dropped me through. This was easy to learn after one fall, and I would call it controllable. Gear is remembered at rest (still "gear 4" after stopping), so tapping Right again re-accelerates to the fast pace almost at once. That could surprise a voter who thinks "stopped means reset".

### 3. Speed drops a step mid-air on long jumps. Noticed? Intended or bug?
I noticed, and it read as intended. In one captured frame (`pt\e4-59-j0.png`) there is a white pulse ring around the player and the background flashes lighter grey, and my colour changes from red to yellow mid-flight (state agrees: red band at take-off, yellow band at landing). The ring/flash makes it clearly deliberate and not a glitch. The catch is that it is a surprise on first contact. I had no warning that long air time would cost me a step, and it could land me on a platform of the wrong colour. A tooltip or tutorial level should introduce it. One caveat: I could only catch one mid-air frame, so I cannot say how long the effect lasts at real-time speed.

## Readability: background and tiles
- Contrast is good. Grey-blue solid tiles with light outlines against a dark navy/near-black background are clear at a glance.
- The background tints toward your pace colour (navy when blue, neutral grey when yellow, maroon on death). It is atmospheric and helps, but the tint at yellow was nearly neutral grey, so the background does not distinguish it well.
- Solid versus fall-through is readable: solid means bright fill, fall-through means a dim dashed outline over the dark background. The dashed state is subtle, though. At a distance the dim yellow dashed tiles nearly vanish into the dark, which is probably the intended "ghost", but a colour-blind or low-brightness player might miss it. The distinct patterns (lines, dots, chevrons, crosshatch) save it.
- The grey goal box is low-contrast against the grey-blue tiles and hard to read as a goal on first sight.

## Scores (as a jam voter), 1 to 5
- Overall: 3. Pace-is-colour is clear and satisfying once it clicks, but the early level asks too much.
- Gameplay: 3.5. The shift/brake/jump interplay is genuinely interesting. The tiny jump is odd.
- Graphics: 3. Clean and readable greybox. The patterned colour cues are smart. Plain, as expected for a greybox.
- Audio: n/a. I could not judge (headless, no sound heard).
- Innovation: 4. Colour as speed and solidity in one is a neat, original use of the theme.
- Theme (SHIFT): 4. Gear shifts, colour shifts and the mid-air step shift all fit.

## The single change that would most improve the game
Add a small always-visible gear/pace indicator (for example a row of coloured pips with the current one lit, plus the colour of tiles you can stand on) so a first-time player can see "I am gear 1 of 4, and red needs gear 4". Several of my deaths came from guessing what pace the next colour needed.

## Smaller suggestions
- Show controls on screen for the first few seconds. The itch blurb is the only place they are given.
- Make the goal pocket easier to read as the goal.
- Hint that shifting works in the air.
- Fix the meaningless "steps" number on the win banner.
