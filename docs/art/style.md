# Redshift: Visual Direction

_Owner: art-director. One page for later work (rendering, effects, itch.io art). Values live in `src/data/palette.json`; accessibility rules and numbers in `docs/art/accessibility.md`. The three directions considered are in `docs/art/style-frames/README.md`; the studio owner picked **C, Pixel Dither** on 2026-11-03. Rendering approach: `docs/tech/adr-pixel-rendering.md` (480 x 270 base, integer zoom)._

## Look

Chunky pixel art on a near-black field: 16 px tiles, 12 x 24 px bodies, 1 px outlines, ordered (Bayer) dither for glow and fades, a 5 x 7 bitmap font. Everything sits on the 480 x 270 grid, so no half pixels. The world's hue comes from the player's pace; the tiles stay true to the palette. Think a late-night arcade cabinet, not a CRT filter (no scanlines: they would eat ghost contrast).

## The spectrum tint (pace is color)

The whole world's tint slides along the spectrum with the player's **actual** speed. It is a continuous cue; gameplay still uses the discrete bands.

- Stops (`palette.tint.stops`): standing 0 white, 0.25 blue, 0.5 green, 0.75 yellow, 1.0 red, in normalized speed (`|vx| / gearSpeeds[4]`), interpolated in sRGB by `spectrumTint` (`src/fx/color.ts`). `tintForSpeed(speed, topSpeed)` does the lookup. Smooth the speed (ease about 100 ms) before lookup so the tint never flickers at a band edge.
- The flat **field** (camera background) is `backgroundForSpeed(speed, topSpeed)`: the pace hue at a fixed luminance cap, 0.013 (`tint.maxBackgroundLuminance`), scaled by saturation so standing stays the near-black `#0B0D14` (to within a step). Yellow is now 17.7 delta-E from standing (it was 9.5, "near-neutral grey").
- The rest of the sky takes the hue through **dithered layers** (below) so the world is visibly tinted without lifting the flat field behind tiles. Every decor colour stays at or under the cap (stars at most 0.03, single pixels).
- Never mix the raw tint into the field: white at speed 0 turns it grey (the old bug, `accessibility.md` section 7).

## Assets: the final format (the contract for scene, ui and tech-lead)

Everything is generated by this repo's own code (nothing third-party; MIT, no `CREDITS.md` entry needed). The source of truth is **code in `src/fx/pixel/`** (pure, unit-tested, no Phaser). `node assets/art/pixel/build.mjs` bakes it to the files below and writes upscaled previews to `docs/art/pixel/`. Tests fail if a committed file differs from the code by one pixel (`src/fx/pixel/sheets.test.ts`, `font.test.ts`), so rerun the script after any change under `src/fx/pixel/` or to `palette.json`. All sheets are RGBA PNG, loaded with nearest-neighbour filtering (`pixelArt: true`).

**Why files and not boot-time textures.** The ADR (`docs/tech/adr-pixel-rendering.md`) planned `buildPixelTextures(scene)` and an 18 x 26 `player.png`. The format here is different on purpose: one sheet per kind, baked from code with the palette already applied, loaded with `load.atlas` / `load.bitmapFont` / `load.image`. The ADR's real concerns all hold: even frame width (20), origin on a pixel boundary (feet at an integer row, x at 10.0), 1 px transparent gutter, no trimming or rotation, tiny memory (about 2.6 MB of GPU textures in total, the largest sheet is 1009 x 204). Colours are not hand-painted into PNGs: they come from `palette.json` through the bakers, and a palette change is one script run plus a commit (the drift tests make forgetting it impossible). The bakers are also exported (`bakePlayerFrame`, `bakeSafeTile`, ...), so the scene may build the same textures at boot instead; the pixels are identical. Keeping one path (load the files) is the recommendation.

| Phaser key | Files in `assets/art/pixel/` | Loader | Contents |
|---|---|---|---|
| `pixel-player` | `pixel-player.png` + `.json` | `load.atlas` | 290 frames: 2 bodies x 5 bands x 29 |
| `pixel-tiles` | `pixel-tiles.png` + `.json` | `load.atlas` | safe, solid and ghost tiles, exit door and glow (named frames) |
| `pixel-tileset` | `pixel-tileset.png` (+ `.json` index summary, not loaded) | `load.image`, then `tilemap.addTilesetImage` | the same tiles as a 16 x 10 grid for Tilemap layers |

| `pixel-hud` | `pixel-hud.png` + `.json` | `load.atlas` | silhouette glyphs, home glyph, edge arrows |

Keys are exported as `PIXEL_KEYS` from `src/fx`. Import the files with Vite (`import url from '.../pixel-player.png?url'`).

| frames `stand`, `run-0..3`, `jump`, `fall`, `twin-*`, `exit` | `idle_0..1`, `run_0..5`, `sprint_0..5`, `jump_0..1`, `fall_0..1`, `land_0..1`, `skid_0..1`, `death_0..3`, `park_0`, `shift_0..1`; body `twin` instead of the `twin-` prefix; exit is `tile/exit/door_0..3` and `tile/exit/glow_0..3` in `pixel-tiles` | animation names, frame counts and suggested fps are in `PLAYER_ANIMS` and in the atlas JSON under `redshift.anims` |

HUD atlas frames (`hudFrameNames()`): `glyph/runner`, `glyph/twin` (7 x 12 white masks, tint at runtime), `glyph/home` (7 x 7 white mask), `hud/edge/<runner|twin>/<band>` (22 x 16, points right, stored pivot (0, 0.5), `flipX` at the left edge). Scenery frames: `bg/horizon`, `bg/stars`, `bg/ridge_far`, `bg/ridge_near`; tint with `sceneryColors(speed, topSpeed)`.

### Player frame geometry

- **Cell 20 x 28**, even width. The 12 x 24 body sits at x 4 to 15 (centre x 10.0); frames face right, flip for left.
- **Pivot (0.5, 26/28)** is stored per frame in the JSON, so Phaser's sprite origin is the feet: `sprite.setPosition(round(feetX_px), round(feetY_px))` puts the bottom of the body on the floor line. Body rows end at row 25; the 1 px keyline under the feet is row 26 and overlaps the floor's top row by design (draw bodies after tiles). Both numbers are integers, so no half pixels at any zoom.
- **Gutter** 1 px transparent between frames, no trim, no rotation.
- Animation frame choice stays in the scene lane (pure `playerFrame(player, params)` from sim state, as the ADR says); suggested fps per animation is in `PLAYER_ANIMS`.

### Bitmap font (`pixel-font`)

BMFont XML plus a white-on-transparent PNG, drawn from code (`src/fx/pixel/font.ts`, own drawing, nothing third-party). Face `redshift-5x7`, glyph box 5 x 8 (cap height 7, lowercase x-height 5, row 7 is the descender row of `g j p q y`), **advance 6, line height 8**. Tint it from `palette.ui` (`BitmapText.setTint`); big text is `setScale(2)`. Characters: printable ASCII 32 to 126 plus `FONT_ICONS`:

| Name in `FONT_ICONS` | Char | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `arrowLeft` `arrowUp` `arrowRight` `arrowDown` | `←` `↑` `→` `↓` | `keyLabel` in `src/ui/bindings.ts`, controls screen, hints |
| `shift` | `⇧` | shift keys, gear meter hints |
| `triangleUp` `triangleDown` | `▲` `▼` | gear meter, level select |
| `triangleRight` `triangleLeft` | `▶` `◀` | menus |
| `tick` `hollowDot` `dot` `star` | `✓` `○` `●` `★` | results, level select progress |
| `lock` | U+E000 (private use; there is no lock in the 16-bit range Phaser reads) | locked levels |
| `emDash` `ellipsis` `middot` `times` | `—` `…` `·` `×` | `winText`, labels |

Icons are 5 wide like letters, so ui-engineer needs no separate 7 x 7 icon set for these. `textWidth(text)` gives a line's pixel width (6 per character, minus the 1 px trailing gap). Descenders use the leading row, so for paragraphs of mixed case add 1 px of line spacing. A character the font lacks draws nothing (Phaser skips it): stick to the list. Preview: `docs/art/pixel/font-sample.png`.

## Player (two bodies, Tandem)

Frame geometry, pivot and names are in "Assets: the final format" above.

| Anim | Frames | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `idle` | 2 | second frame blinks and sinks 1 px |
| `run` | 6 | blue and green; play faster with speed (suggested 12 fps) |
| `sprint` | 6 | yellow and red; leans 2 px and stretches (14 fps) |
| `jump` | 2 | crouch, then rise (legs tucked, 1 row of stretch) |
| `fall` | 2 | legs trail, 1 row of stretch |
| `land` | 2 | squash 3 rows, then 1 |
| `skid` | 2 | leans back, feet forward (pairs with a dust puff) |
| `death` | 4 | white flash, crack, four chunks, dithered scatter. Chunks keep their pattern pixels |
| `park` | 1 | home: see below |
| `shift` | 2 | white flash for one frame, then a white-outlined frame: the band-change pulse (see below) |

**How a band change shows.** A frame is a grid of roles (outline, fill, ink, visor, shade, light, keyline), not colours. Switching band swaps the role palette (the band's fill, light outline and ink) and fills the torso pattern cell with the new band's pattern; nothing else in the frame changes, so the swap is exactly one frame (no tween, matching the sim). The `shift` frames are shown for the two frames of a band change (the drag-downshift event uses them too); reduced motion skips them and just swaps.

**Two bodies, told apart without colour.** Runner (body 0): flat head, one wide visor, legs under the torso. Twin (body 1): antenna above the head, chamfered shoulders, two square eyes, wide stance. Each has its own band, so both show their own pattern at all times; a test requires at least 8 pattern pixels on the body in every frame of every marked band, and at least 10 silhouette pixels of difference between runner and twin in every frame. White (standing) has no marks by design; its visor is dark so it still reads. Draw the twin behind the runner when they overlap.

**Home (parked).** When a body gets home it switches to `park_0` and stops animating: a deep crouch (4 rows shorter), legs together, eyes shut, and a small house mark in the HUD text colour hanging above the head, clear of the body. The band pattern stays on the torso. It is one frame for each body and band, so the scene only needs `playerFrameName(body, band, 'park_0')`.

Every body carries a 1 px near-black keyline so it separates from a same-coloured floor.

## Tiles

- **Safe** (always solid): grey `#5F6987`, a lit two-row top lip, dithered darker with depth (5 levels), rivets, light outline on exposed edges. The only plain tile.
- **Solid colored**: fill, lit flat top edge (two light rows plus a shade row: this is the "ledge" cue), the band's pattern in dark ink, shade underside, light outline on exposed edges. `m<mask>` says which neighbours are the same kind (bit 0 up, 1 right, 2 down, 3 left); an edge is outlined only when that neighbour is absent. Safe `d<depth>` is the number of safe tiles above it, capped at 4.
- **Ghost colored**: no fill (a faint dithered wash through transparency), only the rim of the pattern marks in the ghost color, a 1 px outline dashed 3 on, 2 off with solid corners. No lit edge, no shade, no glow.
- **Exit**: white frame (pure `#FFFFFF`, used by nothing else), dark doorway, a four-point sparkle that pulses over `door_0` to `door_3`; `glow_n` is a 32 x 32 dither halo centred on the door tile at alpha about 0.47, kept within 8 px above and below the tile so it never reaches the ghost tiles above. Reduced motion: hold `door_0`, no pulse.
- **Mechanics (mud, ice, boost, gates, conveyors):** not built. They wait for design-lead; the 2026-11-03 sketches in `style-frames/mechanics-strip.png` are neon-direction sketches, to be redrawn at 16 px when the list is final.

## Background layers

White masks that the scene tints at runtime with `sceneryColors(speed, topSpeed)`, so the hue slides continuously:

- `bg/horizon` (480 x 270): ordered-dither glow strongest at y 175, empty by y 55 and y 295. Tint `hue`; alpha `horizonAlpha` (0.6 standing, 1.0 moving).
- `bg/stars`: 46 single pixels in the upper sky. Tint `star`.
- `bg/ridge_far` (top y 128) and `bg/ridge_near` (top y 160): silhouettes with a dithered top edge, 480 px wide and **seamless**: scroll them as repeating tile sprites at different parallax factors. Tints `ridgeFar` and `ridgeNear` are darker than the field, so they never raise the luminance behind a ghost.
- Layer order back to front: camera field colour, horizon, stars, far ridge, near ridge, tiles.

## Effects (pixel versions; render-only, never feed the sim)

- **Afterimages:** three dithered copies of the body (50%, 25%, 12% density) at 10 px steps, each in an earlier band's color (at red: red, orange, yellow). Reduced motion removes them.
- **Speed lines:** 1 px lines in the tint (lightened), dithered out along their length; more and longer with speed. Baked: `fx/speed/<12|20|32>_<a|b>` (W x 1, always lit at the right end, density 100% at the head falling to 15% at the tail, origin (1, 0), `flipX` when moving left; the `a` and `b` phases use different Bayer rows so neighbouring lines share no holes). Show at red (gear 4) only, the longer lines at higher ground speed. Tint: the current band's `solid.outline`.
- **Dust:** 1 to 2 px specks at the feet. Baked: `fx/dust/land_0..3` (24 x 8, origin (0.5, 1) at the feet, 4 frames at 20 fps, play once on the sim's land event; 2 px specks fly out and up in frames 0 and 1, 1 px specks and thinning dither in 2 and 3). Tint `ui.textMuted`.
- **Ring burst** on a band change: `fx/ring/0..3` (48 x 48, origin (0.5, 0.5) on the body, 4 steps at 25 fps, play once; a 2 px dithered ring at radius 8, 13, 18, 22 and lit density 100%, 75%, 50%, 30%). Tint: the new band's `solid.outline`. It covers under 15% of its frame, so it is never a flash.
- **Effect sheet:** all of the above are white masks in atlas `pixel-fx` (`PIXEL_KEYS.fx`), pure `#FFFFFF` at full alpha with fades done by dither, so a tint is one colour swap. Previews: `docs/art/pixel/effects-yellow.png` and `effects-red.png`. Frame names: `effectFrameNames()`; the atlas JSON carries `redshift.anims` and `redshift.reducedMotion`.
- **Reduced motion:** ring burst off (palette swap only); speed lines off; dust held on the static frame `fx/dust/land_1` for 4 sim steps, no spread; afterimages off.
- **Drag-downshift event:** the sim's `bandChange` while airborne to a lower band: the `shift` frames, a two-frame dither wipe over the screen at `palette.flash.alpha`, the clunk SFX on the same frame. Reduced motion: palette swap only (no wipe, no ring). Never more than 3 flashes a second.
- **Reduced effects** turns off the horizon dither, stars, speed lines and afterimages (the landing dust and the ring stay unless reduced motion is also on); tiles, sprites and the field stay. There is no post-processing to turn off in this direction.

## HUD

The 220 x 64 (110 x 32 art pixels) gear meter and the timer use the bitmap font (system fonts antialias and then get scaled). The frames in `style-frames/pixel-*.png` show the intended look: a dark `ui.panel` with a 1 px border tinted by the pace, the lit cell filled with its pattern, a bracket on the selected gear, digits 1 to 4 below, a needle. The HUD itself belongs to the ui lane; this doc only fixes the colors and the patterns (`PIXEL_CELLS.hud` is 12 x 8). Text sits on a panel, never directly on art.

## Tandem art (T-05)

- **Silhouettes.** Runner: flat head, one wide visor, legs under the torso. Twin: antenna, chamfered shoulders, two square eyes, wide stance. Design-lead accepted this with no extra marker (2026-11-04); a marker only if M3-34 asks. Draw the twin behind the runner.
- **HUD strip glyphs.** `glyph/runner` and `glyph/twin` (7 x 12) are the same shapes reduced to a mask: the twin glyph has the one-pixel antenna and two eye holes, the runner glyph a wide visor gap. They fit the 14 px strips with 1 px above and below. Tint with the body's band fill, or with `ui.textMuted` for a parked strip. A parked strip also shows `glyph/home` (7 x 7).
- **Camera edge arrow.** `hud/edge/<body>/<band>` is a 22 x 16 right-pointing arrow: 1 px `ui.text` outline, `ui.panel` fill, the body's silhouette inside in the band's fill colour. Flip it at the left edge. The outline and silhouette say "that body is over there"; the colour only says which gear it is in. It is baked for both bodies and all five bands.
- **Lane divider.** There is no special tile: the divider is ordinary safe tiles (a slab at least 1 tile thick). A one-tile slab reads as solid ground (lit lip, outline top and bottom, rivets, the only plain grey tile), and it is the one thing in the lane that is neither pattern nor colour, so bodies in both lanes stand on the same kind of floor. Checked in `docs/art/pixel/tandem-scene.png`.
- **Checks.** Runner against twin in greyscale and the three colour-vision simulations, with numbers: `docs/art/accessibility.md` section 10 (image: `docs/art/pixel/tandem-vision.png`).
