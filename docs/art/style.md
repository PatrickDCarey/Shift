# Redshift: Visual Direction

_Owner: art-director. One page for later work (rendering, effects, itch.io art). Values live in `src/data/palette.json`; accessibility rules and numbers in `docs/art/accessibility.md`. The three directions considered are in `docs/art/style-frames/README.md`; the studio owner picked **C, Pixel Dither** on 2026-11-03. Rendering approach: `docs/tech/adr-pixel-rendering.md` (480 x 270 base, integer zoom)._

## Look

Chunky pixel art on a near-black field: 16 px tiles, 12 x 24 px bodies, 1 px outlines, ordered (Bayer) dither for glow and fades, a 5 x 7 bitmap font. Everything sits on the 480 x 270 grid, so no half pixels. The world's hue comes from the player's pace; the tiles stay true to the palette. Think a late-night arcade cabinet, not a CRT filter (no scanlines: they would eat ghost contrast).

## The spectrum tint (pace is color)

The whole world's tint slides along the spectrum with the player's **actual** speed. It is a continuous cue; gameplay still uses the discrete bands.

- Stops (`palette.tint.stops`): standing 0 white, 0.25 blue, 0.5 green, 0.75 yellow, 1.0 red, in normalized speed (`|vx| / gearSpeeds[4]`), interpolated in sRGB by `spectrumTint` (`src/fx/color.ts`). `tintForSpeed(speed, topSpeed)` does the lookup. Smooth the speed (ease about 100 ms) before lookup so the tint never flickers at a band edge.
- The flat **field** (camera background) is `backgroundForSpeed(speed, topSpeed)`: the pace hue at a fixed luminance cap, 0.013 (`tint.maxBackgroundLuminance`), scaled by saturation so standing stays the near-black `#0B0D14` (to within a step). Yellow is now 17.7 delta-E from standing (it was 9.5, "near-neutral grey").
- The rest of the sky takes the hue through **dithered layers** (below) so the world is visibly tinted without lifting the flat field behind tiles. Every decor colour stays at or under the cap (stars at most 0.03, single pixels).
- Never mix the raw tint into the field: white at speed 0 turns it grey (the old bug, `accessibility.md` section 7).

## Assets: what is PNG and what is code

Everything is generated by this repo's own code (nothing third-party; MIT, no `CREDITS.md` entry needed). The source of truth is **code in `src/fx/pixel/`** (pure, unit-tested); `node assets/art/pixel/build.mjs` bakes it to PNG sheets plus Phaser JSON-hash atlases in `assets/art/pixel/`, and a test fails if the committed atlases drift from the code. The scene can load the PNGs (`this.load.atlas`) or call the bakers at boot; both give identical pixels.

## Player (two bodies, Tandem)

Frames are 20 x 28 with the 12 x 24 body at x 4 to 15 and its feet on row 26; sprite origin is (0.5, 26/28), which the atlas stores as the frame pivot. Frames face right; flip for left.

| Anim | Frames | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `idle` | 2 | second frame blinks and sinks 1 px |
| `run` | 6 | blue and green; play faster with speed (suggested 12 fps) |
| `sprint` | 6 | yellow and red; leans 2 px and stretches (14 fps) |
| `jump` | 2 | crouch, then rise (legs tucked, 1 row of stretch) |
| `fall` | 2 | legs trail, 1 row of stretch |
| `land` | 2 | squash 3 rows, then 1 |
| `skid` | 2 | leans back, feet forward (pairs with a dust puff) |
| `death` | 4 | white flash, crack, four chunks, dithered scatter. Chunks keep their pattern pixels |
| `shift` | 2 | white flash for one frame, then a white-outlined frame: the band-change pulse (see below) |

**How a band change shows.** A frame is a grid of roles (outline, fill, ink, visor, shade, light, keyline), not colours. Switching band swaps the role palette (the band's fill, light outline and ink) and fills the torso pattern cell with the new band's pattern; nothing else in the frame changes, so the swap is exactly one frame (no tween, matching the sim). The `shift` frames are shown for the two frames of a band change (the drag-downshift event uses them too); reduced motion skips them and just swaps.

**Two bodies, told apart without colour.** Runner (body 0): flat head, one wide visor, legs under the torso. Twin (body 1): antenna above the head, chamfered shoulders, two square eyes, wide stance. Each has its own band, so both show their own pattern at all times; a test requires at least 8 pattern pixels on the body in every frame of every marked band, and at least 10 silhouette pixels of difference between runner and twin in every frame. White (standing) has no marks by design; its visor is dark so it still reads. Draw the twin behind the runner when they overlap.

Every body carries a 1 px near-black keyline so it separates from a same-coloured floor.

## Tiles

- **Safe** (always solid): grey `#5F6987`, a lit two-row top lip, dithered darker with depth (5 levels), rivets, light outline on exposed edges. The only plain tile.
- **Solid colored**: fill, lit flat top edge (two light rows plus a shade row: this is the "ledge" cue), the band's pattern in dark ink, shade underside, light outline on exposed edges. `m<mask>` says which neighbours are the same kind (bit 0 up, 1 right, 2 down, 3 left); an edge is outlined only when that neighbour is absent. Safe `d<depth>` is the number of safe tiles above it, capped at 4.
- **Ghost colored**: no fill (a faint dithered wash through transparency), only the rim of the pattern marks in the ghost color, a 1 px outline dashed 3 on, 2 off with solid corners. No lit edge, no shade, no glow.
- **Exit**: white frame (pure `#FFFFFF`, used by nothing else), dark doorway, a four-point sparkle that pulses over `door_0` to `door_3`; `glow_n` is a 32 x 32 dither halo centred on the door tile at alpha about 0.47, kept within 8 px above and below the tile so it never reaches the ghost tiles above. Reduced motion: hold `door_0`, no pulse.
- **Mechanics (mud, ice, boost, gates, conveyors):** not built. They wait for design-lead; the 2026-11-03 sketches in `style-frames/mechanics-strip.png` are neon-direction sketches, to be redrawn at 16 px when the list is final.

## Background layers

White masks that the scene tints at runtime with `sceneryColors(speed, topSpeed)`, so the hue slides continuously:

- `bg/horizon` (480 x 270): ordered-dither glow strongest at y 175, empty by y 55 and y 295. Tint `hue`; alpha `horizonAlpha` (0.6 standing, 1.0 moving).
- `bg/stars`: 46 single pixels in the upper sky. Tint `star`.
- `bg/ridge_far` (top y 128) and `bg/ridge_near` (top y 160): silhouettes with a dithered top edge, 480 px wide and **seamless**: scroll them as repeating tile sprites at different parallax factors. Tints `ridgeFar` and `ridgeNear` are darker than the field, so they never raise the luminance behind a ghost.
- Layer order back to front: camera field colour, horizon, stars, far ridge, near ridge, tiles.

## Effects (pixel versions; render-only, never feed the sim)

- **Afterimages:** three dithered copies of the body (50%, 25%, 12% density) at 10 px steps, each in an earlier band's color (at red: red, orange, yellow). Reduced motion removes them.
- **Speed lines:** 1 px lines in the tint (lightened), dithered out along their length; more and longer with speed.
- **Dust:** 1 to 2 px specks at the feet. **Ring burst** on a drag downshift: dithered ring, 4 steps (to be baked when the effect is wired).
- **Drag-downshift event:** the sim's `bandChange` while airborne to a lower band: the `shift` frames, a two-frame dither wipe over the screen at `palette.flash.alpha`, the clunk SFX on the same frame. Reduced motion: palette swap only (no wipe, no ring). Never more than 3 flashes a second.
- **Reduced effects** turns off the horizon dither, stars and afterimages; tiles, sprites and the field stay. There is no post-processing to turn off in this direction.

## HUD

The 220 x 64 (110 x 32 art pixels) gear meter and the timer use the bitmap font (system fonts antialias and then get scaled). The frames in `style-frames/pixel-*.png` show the intended look: a dark `ui.panel` with a 1 px border tinted by the pace, the lit cell filled with its pattern, a bracket on the selected gear, digits 1 to 4 below, a needle. The HUD itself belongs to the ui lane; this doc only fixes the colors and the patterns (`PIXEL_CELLS.hud` is 12 x 8). Text sits on a panel, never directly on art.
